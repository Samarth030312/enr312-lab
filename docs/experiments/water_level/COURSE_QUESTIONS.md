# ENR312 WATER handout: how this set-up answers every task and question

Source: ENR312 *Water Level Control Experiment (WATER)* guidelines, SRC-014 (not redistributed).
Apparatus: Arduino Uno, **HC-SR04** ultrasonic sensor above the tank, small DC pump switched by a
**relay module** (optional logic-level MOSFET on D5 for P/PI), optional float switch for overflow,
wiring in [`WATER_LEVEL_WIRING.md`](../../wiring/WATER_LEVEL_WIRING.md). Tank and pump dimensions are
ASSUMED until measured (S3-2, S3-3).

Status: **DONE-SIM** ([`COURSE_SIM_RESULTS.md`](COURSE_SIM_RESULTS.md), generated by
`matlab/scripts/water_course_questions.m`), **PENDING-HW** = `enr312_start water`: connect, mission and preset, RUN ON RIG.
The analysis functions used on simulated data (`enr312.level.cycleAnalysis`, `enr312.level.calibrate`)
are the ones to run on the raw hardware files.

## Section 2 — questions (reference answers)

1. **Purpose.** Keep a liquid level within limits automatically: supply without overflow or running dry,
   protect pumps, keep process conditions (pressure head, residence time) constant.
2. **Examples.** Overhead domestic water tanks; boiler drums; water treatment and sumps; chemical/process
   vessels; cooling towers; irrigation reservoirs; toilet cistern float valve.
3. **Why feedback?** Inflow and outflow change (usage, supply pressure, pump wear); without measuring the
   level the controller cannot know, and a timed pump would overflow or empty the tank.
4. **Open vs closed loop.** Open loop acts on a schedule without measuring the result (pump for 2 min).
   Closed loop measures the output (level) and corrects the input from the error.
5. **P, I, D.** P: output proportional to the present error. I: proportional to the accumulated error
   over time. D: proportional to the rate of change of the error.
6. **P leaves an error.** To keep pumping the flow that balances the outflow the pump needs a non-zero
   command, and with u = Kp·e that requires a non-zero error (S3-10).
7. **I removes it.** The integral keeps growing while any error remains, so it can supply the needed pump
   command with e = 0; the level settles exactly at the setpoint.
8. **High D gain.** Amplifies sensor noise (ultrasonic ripples) into large, jumpy pump commands; wear and
   possible instability. Needs filtering.
9. **First-order tank.** H(s)/Q_in(s) = K/(τs + 1), with K = 2√h0/k, τ = 2A√h0/k after linearising the
   gravity outflow (S3-9). With a constant outflow: H(s)/Q_in(s) = 1/(A s) (integrator).
10. **Linear-model assumptions.** Small deviations around one operating level (√h linearised), constant
    cross-section, incompressible water, outflow depending only on level, pump flow proportional to the
    command, no delays, no saturation.
11. **Level sensors.** Ultrasonic (HC-SR04), float switches, capacitive or conductive probes, pressure
    (hydrostatic) sensors, optical/IR, radar, sight glass.
12. **Ultrasonic.** Sends a 40 kHz burst and times the echo from the surface: d = v·t/2, level =
    sensor height − d (S3-1). v depends on air temperature.
13. **PWM for the pump.** Varies the average voltage, hence speed and flow, efficiently through a MOSFET:
    needed for P/PI; a relay can only switch fully on/off.
14. **Arduino's role.** Reads the sensor, computes the error and the control law every 0.1 s, drives the
    relay/MOSFET, enforces the safety interlocks and reports to the PC.
15. **Dry-run protection.** Pump only if the reservoir has water (float/level switch in the sump), and stop
    the pump if commanded hard for a set time without the tank level rising (implemented: 60 s, S3-11);
    current sensing or flow switch as alternatives.
16. **Why calibrate?** Mounting height, tank geometry, temperature (speed of sound) and sensor offset
    differ per rig; without calibration Lmin/Lmax are not where you think they are (S3-1, S3-2).
17. **Unfiltered noise.** Random readings cross the thresholds: relay chattering, pump starts, wear;
    jumpy P/PI output; possible false trips of guards. Firmware uses a median of 3 pings.
18. **Safety.** Keep water away from electronics and mains; 12 V pump supply with fuse; secure the tank;
    overflow tray; relay rated for the pump; never run the pump dry; switch off before touching wiring.
19. **Simulate first.** Choose thresholds and gains safely, see cycle times, find chattering and windup,
    size tank/pump, test the interlock logic without flooding the bench.
20. **Tank in Simulink.** Sum (Q_in − Q_out) → Gain 1/A → Integrator (limits 0…H) → level; outflow from a
    constant or k√h; Relay block for ON-OFF; Discrete PID for P/PI; Zero-Order Hold + Quantizer for the sensor.
21. **Process gain, time constant.** K: steady change in level per unit change in inflow. τ: time to reach
    63 % of that change after a step (S3-9).
22. **Disturbance rejection.** Real tanks see changing outflows (taps opened); the controller must hold
    the level, not just follow setpoint changes (S3-8: extra drain).
23. **Windup.** When the pump is saturated (u = 255) the integral keeps growing, causing overshoot and slow
    recovery later. Simulink PID blocks offer anti-windup "clamping" (stop integrating at the limit) or
    "back-calculation" (S3-8 shows both behaviours).
24. **Noise and PID.** Noise enters through the measurement: P and especially D turn it into actuator
    activity; with gains high enough it excites oscillation; filtering trades noise against delay.
25. **Simulation vs hardware.** S3-12: pump flow vs head, delays, ripples/noise, non-constant outflow,
    speed of sound — fill in with measured evidence.
